# Bytefray V6 — E2–E8 Synthesis and Next-Design Review

**Status:** Research synthesis and design review only, 2026-10-01. E2–E8 remain sealed. No E9 design, preregistration, implementation, experiment or product promotion is authorized by this document.

## 1. Executive summary

The emerging interpretation is supported with two important limits: the positive gameplay findings concern specific scripted populations, and repeated reacquisition is not evidence that changing policy within a match improves payoff. E8 makes the next question different. The program now has a characterized information/action structure worth asking a more capable adaptive agent to exploit; another new mechanic is not presently justified.

- E2 created a response window, but much of its benefit became phase-locked recovery or tick-limit stasis. It did not establish the desired strategic opponent dependence.
- E3 increased mutual action and reduced disruption exclusivity and seat privilege, while exposing continuous-repair immunity. Its registered interpretation table returned no causal row.
- E4 changed ownership dynamics without changing any primary F1 outcome class. E5 showed that anchor/core co-location was unnecessary for the predominant opening-exchange privilege. Neither made that forensic branch a gameplay candidate.
- E6 was the strategic pivot: priced passive sensing produced opponent-dependent best policies and an information/action tradeoff in one nine-member family at arena 512, radius 32. Its seat pathology still required gameplay rejection.
- The E7 audit explained that pathology as a real unit-level interaction involving lockout and ADAPT's schedule, not a stable family-level interaction. It closed with no E7 experiment and did not rescue E6.
- E8 preserved opponent dependence when acquisition became an explicit sensing action, separated from locomotion. This supports active sensing as a viable design channel within the registered ecology.
- Repeated reacquisition paid against EVADE8 under the whole-tick parent and did not under the one-offer companion. This is evidence bounded by disruption economics, not an independent, universal benefit of active sensing.
- E8 cleared its primary kill criteria and had no flagged unit seat transitions. Family-level H8-SEAT was nevertheless NEITHER; this is not a proof of seat neutrality or scheduler independence.
- ADAPT8 varied its verification allocation, but its mixed-field payoff comparison was NEITHER. Requirement C remains NOT ESTABLISHED, rather than contradicted.
- Recommend **BRANCH A EXPERIMENT NEXT**, specifically the Branch A′ form with the existing E8 research mechanics held fixed. Review this synthesis first; only subsequent authorization should begin a focused adaptation design review. No E9 is started here.

The earlier A–G classifications remain unchanged. What changes is their evidential content: A, B, E and G no longer rest only on movement-priced sensing, and the unresolved adaptation question has a better controlled environment. D and F remain PARTIAL for broader interaction quality and human/product explanation. The experiment-level E8 disposition remains **CANDIDATE, for a further design phase**, with promotion **false**.

## 3. E2–E8 progression

### E2 — a response window without the intended strategic resolution

E2 changed capture hold from K=1 to K=2, over 15,360 registered matches. The extra tick removed immediate fatality and made a response possible. H1 was Supported, but H0 was Unsupported globally and H2 only Partially supported; H3a was Partially supported, H3b/H3c/H3f/H3g Supported, and H3d/H3e Unsupported. These are E2's original labels, not retrospective translations into later SUPPORTED/REFUTED terminology.

In primary F1, 1,066 of 1,884 originally decisive cells kept the same winner exactly one tick later. There were 610 new recovery tick-limit cells, comprising 448 ties and 162 score losses; every recovering entrant in that set had phase-lock at least 0.95. The guarded-painter reversals did not involve core capture. Pure repair guards still had 0/64 recoveries against the sniper despite the nominal window. Tick-limit share rose from roughly one third to over one half.

Thus the useful causal contribution was a response window. The deterministic cyclic/draw observations did not establish a structural, robust best-response ecology; seat privilege and recovery pathology persisted. H2's partial result must not be restated as a clean registered negative. K=2 was useful research leverage, not a qualified gameplay solution. [E2 results](V6_E2_CAPTURE_HOLD_RESULTS.md), [E2–E5 synthesis](V6_E2_E5_CROSS_EXPERIMENT_SYNTHESIS.md).

### E3 — more interaction, residual timing and repair immunity

E3 changed whole-tick disruption into loss of the next action offer, λ=1, primarily with K=2 and with a K=1 companion, over 19,456 matches. D2, D3, D6 and D9 were SUPPORTED; D0, D4, D5 and D7 REFUTED; D1 and D8 NEITHER. No registered interpretation row applied. Descriptive causal observations must not be promoted into a row that the registered mapping did not select.

Disruption exclusivity fell from 0.622 to zero, and the mean ticks in which both entrants acted rose from 2.50 to 7.37. Five of six seat-dependent units disappeared, all three F1 ones did, and maximum bias fell from 1 to 0.0625. These are substantive increases in interaction, not simply delayed termination. D7's refutation also rules out the registered broad draw-ification reading; the tick-limit increase was 0.051.

Limits matter. D6's minimal-guard characterization had one distinct trajectory per seat, not broad evidence about continuous repair. D3's 192 guarded losses becoming non-losses all became ties and involved only six distinct trajectories. D9 supported the K=2 × λ=1 continuous-repair immunity: no capture completions, whereas the K=1 companion had 192. Residual last-mover behavior remained mixed. The addendum corrects the guard mirror from 20/12 to **19 Seat A / 13 Seat B**; the twin mirror's SDI=1 was constructed from decisive seeds, not independent seat-consistency evidence. [E3 results and addendum](V6_E3_SLOT_LIMITED_DISRUPTION_RESULTS.md).

### E4 — ownership changes masked by the primary capture regime

E4 mirrored later pass order on the E3 parent, retaining K=2, λ=1 primary and K=1 companion, over 15,232 matches. H1, H3 and H4 were SUPPORTED; H0, H5, H6 and H8 REFUTED; H2 and H7 NEITHER; D9′ HOLDS. No registered causal row applied, including after the interpretation clarifications. NEITHER is not a substitute for the refutation needed by a row.

Mirroring neutralized 16/18 multi-pass ownership units, including 14 substantive cases and two boundary cases. Yet **0/2,304 primary F1 outcome classes changed**. There were only 41/3,808 outcome changes across all primary strata. The K=1 masking companion changed 128/640 outcomes where the corresponding primary subset changed none. Among the opening exchange cases, 13/14 privileges stayed, and all 14 were second-mover swing privileges.

This closes the simple account that the final chunk alone explains primary winner privilege. It does not show that pass order or end-of-tick sampling is irrelevant: ownership changed, and the companion exposed consequential changes. Capture/repair masking and the opening response were the remaining useful explanations. [E4 results](V6_E4_MIRRORED_PASS_ORDER_RESULTS.md).

### E5 — co-location unnecessary; the forensic branch closes

E5 moved initial anchors from core base to the preceding address, on the **E3 forward-pass parent**, over 7,168 matches and seven fixtures. E4 and E5 were sibling interventions; E5 did not stack separation onto E4's mirrored passes.

E5-D PASS and H2 SUPPORTED selected **R-H2-PRIME**: 13/17 sweep-backed privileges stayed. H1 was NEITHER, with four neutralizations; H3 REFUTED, H4/H5 NEITHER and D9 HOLDS. The exceptions prevent an absolute claim. The all-neutralized 15/15 anchor-only set is useful descriptive evidence that removing contact matters, not evidence that every opening privilege vanished.

Combined with E4, this supports the second response in the opening exchange as the surviving mechanism and shows that anchor/core co-location is unnecessary in the registered supermajority. The treatment lost 190/320 captures: 126 became the same winner by score and 64 ties, with no reversed winner. Stasis and new immunity led to research-lead rejection as a gameplay candidate. The anchor/core forensic line was closed. The next question changed to strategic action value rather than another fixture search along that line. [E5 results](V6_E5_ANCHOR_CORE_SEPARATION_RESULTS.md), [E2–E5 synthesis](V6_E2_E5_CROSS_EXPERIMENT_SYNTHESIS.md).

### E6 — the transition to opponent-dependent action economics

E6 priced passive sensing by reducing discovery radius from unlimited to 32 at arena 512, while READ/WRITE retained declared reach. Movement/search consequently consumed actions competing with attack, repair and painting. It used a separate K=1, Q=8, chunk=2 rotating-forward branch and nine scripted members, over 11,520 matches. It did not inherit the entire E2–E5 treatment sequence.

E6-D PASS; H1T SUPPORTED, H1C REFUTED, H2 SUPPORTED; H0 NEITHER and H3 REFUTED. **R-CREATES**, qualified by H2, says that in this family and geometry priced sensing made the best policy depend on the opponent. The control's SPLIT was universal; the treatment had no universal best policy. Lower-acquisition contrasts also supported a real information/action tradeoff. H0's NEITHER prevents calling the entire result a registered refutation of the timing-tax account.

That strategic result survives the disposition: **REJECT as a gameplay candidate**, because KC-5/PF-4 identified the new PACED–ADAPT seat artifact. PF-1 through PF-3 were not raised. Requirement C was not established; ADAPT was not a primary demonstration of adaptive benefit. Controls had few distinct trajectories, so the precise scripted family scope is essential. Seed reconstruction remained a product blocker even though the research family and blind protocol forbade using it.

The governing family freeze followed Amendment 1 before seeds: restrict unverified adoption to Seat A's first callback, confirm core cells without the agent's own disruption invalidating its probe, and use a cyclic core cursor to prevent starvation of cell 7. These are qualification/family corrections, not post-result gameplay rescue. They are preserved in E8's inherited posture behavior. [E6 preregistration](V6_E6_PRICED_SENSING_PREREGISTRATION.md), [implementation record](V6_E6_IMPLEMENTATION_RECORD.md), [final results](V6_E6_PRICED_SENSING_RESULTS.md), [E2–E6 synthesis](V6_E2_E6_CROSS_EXPERIMENT_SYNTHESIS.md).

### E7 — audit closure, not a seventh experiment

The sensing × disruption design review and subsequent post-hoc audit used E6's existing corpus. They produced no fresh registration, matrix, seeds or gameplay matches. The approved closure is **NO NEW EXPERIMENT**.

The durable reading is a real unit-level interaction involving priced sensing, whole-tick lockout, and ADAPT's absolute-tick/callback-sensitive schedule, **not evidence of a stable family-level sensing × disruption interaction**. Lockout was necessary in the PACED–ADAPT mechanism but insufficient: PACED–GUARD remained neutral despite GUARD being locked out.

Neither is confirmatory experiment evidence or a general benefit of whole-tick disruption. ## 4. What E8 changes, and what it does not

**Channel independence within this design.** Primary treatment P_none and Q_none were true with 994/1,000 registered draws, and control P_none with 900/1,000. Alongside CHANNEL and LESS, this supports an explicit information channel that preserves opponent-dependent choice without requiring movement to acquire information. It is stronger than an accidental movement/search result. It does not establish that active sensing creates the strategic structure independently of the inherited disruption, geometry, knowledge and family choices. R8-PRESERVES is preservation, not R8-CREATES. [E8 registered payoff evidence](V6_E8_RESULTS.md).

**Repeated value, bounded by the parent.** Against the census {EVADE8}, the repeat-versus-once contrast ΔR was 31/64 under whole-tick disruption, at or above the registered effect threshold in all 1,000 draws. In the companion ΔR was zero, nonpositive in all 1,000. That is a strong registered contrast between these two ecologies. It supports the design account that reacquisition becomes worthwhile when successful renewed disruption can deny substantial action capacity. It is not a new factorial estimate proving that disruption economics is the sole cause of every difference.

The preregistration and final report preserve the sentence:

> If H8-REPEAT succeeds under the whole-tick parent, E8 demonstrates repeated adaptation in an ecology where disruption makes reacquisition valuable. It does not establish that active sensing creates repeated adaptation independently of disruption economics.

That registered language remains unchanged. For requirements assessment, read it alongside the preregistration's explicit separation of repeated reacquisition from C. REACQ8 repeats a fixed verification/search rule; successful repetition is not itself an adaptive policy switch. [Preregistration §§1, 7.3–7.4](V6_E8_ACTIVE_SPATIAL_SENSING_PREREGISTRATION.md).

**A candidate clears the specified pathology screen.** Primary tick-limit share fell from 613/1760 to 401/1760, no-hostile-core-contact share from 213/1760 to zero, and capture share rose from 1147/1760 to 1359/1760. There was no new immune member. These are meaningful registered preservation results, not proof that all matches discover one another or that future agents cannot stall. Detection and hostile core contact are different measures; the registered no-discovery tables retain cases without discovery. The companion tick-limit share increased, from 1369/3520 to 753/1760, without raising PF8-1. Preserve that asymmetry.

**The E6 seat failure did not recur as an E8 primary flag.** E8's unit transition rule includes registered stability requirements, and its family is different. [Seat/pathology layers](V6_E8_RESULTS.md), [preregistration seat criteria](V6_E8_ACTIVE_SPATIAL_SENSING_PREREGISTRATION.md).

**Adaptation has become the narrower strategic question.** Primary U(ADAPT8)=287/352 versus best fixed 9/11; its support predicate held in 415/1,000 draws and its refutation predicate in zero. This is NEITHER, not a registered finding of damaging adaptation. The best-fixed set in that comparison is RUSH8 and REACQ8, not every policy a capable agent might implement. ## 6. Four distinct strategic properties

| Property | Current evidence | What the finding means |
|---|---|---|
| Opponent-dependent **fixed-policy choice** | **DEMONSTRATED**, E6/E8 scoped families | Different fixed policies are useful against different opponents. Choosing one before a match is not within-match adaptation. |
| **Information value** | **DEMONSTRATED**, registered acquisition/payoff contrasts | Acquisition and its opportunity cost alter achievable payoff; information is neither always free nor always worth buying. This is not an oracle-versus-no-oracle estimate of all knowledge. |
| **Repeated information value** | **DEMONSTRATED**, {EVADE8}, whole-tick primary only | Re-finding a relocated anchor pays under the registered repeating policy. One-offer companion REFUTED. The anchor moves; the owned core remains fixed. |
| **Within-match adaptation** | **NOT ESTABLISHED** | ADAPT8 changes a policy dimension and varies allocation, but the registered payoff comparison is NEITHER. Behavior variation alone does not establish benefit. |

A fixed algorithm can respond to observations while keeping the same policy: repeat verification when possible, search when an anchor is missing, attack when located. That is stateful execution. C asks whether changing the allocation/policy itself in response to state improves payoff. E8's separate registered tests support maintaining this distinction. [Preregistration §§3.5, 7.2–7.4](V6_E8_ACTIVE_SPATIAL_SENSING_PREREGISTRATION.md).

## 7. Current mechanic-stack assessment

### Structurally useful interactions

Active SENSE costs one offer, examines a circular ±27 window, and returns live enemy anchors at the instant it executes. It does not move an anchor, paint, disrupt, reveal the sensor to the opponent or directly expose enemy cores. Its result reaches the same process's next callback, which can be delayed by lockout. These semantics give information an explicit, competing use of a limited action budget. [Agent API research extension](../../AGENT_API_V2.md#research-only-extension-sense), [E8 family freeze](V6_E8_FAMILY_FREEZE.md).

Remembered contacts, silent evasion and refreshed sensing create a useful loop: locate an anchor, spend to disrupt it, lose its current location when it moves, and choose whether to pay to find it again. Core positions do not move with anchors. Reacquisition principally restores a disruption target rather than relocating an already confirmed enemy core. Attack and repair still compete with information offers; broad acquisition can lose to cheaper acquisition against suitable opponents. This supports a design system with meaningful action allocation, without establishing an adaptive controller for that system.

Movement is no longer mandatory search in active T8: only EVADE8 moves anchors in this frozen family. It remains useful as evasion, while passive controls use MOVE for search. Acquisition and locomotion are separable here, not interchangeable across every other geometry or roster. Passive current visibility versus persistent active memory is an evidence-bearing part of the substitution, not a nuisance to silently equalize.

### Scheduler dependence and promotion risks

Whole-tick disruption supplies substantial denial value after an early hit. One evasion plus a short reacquisition sequence can then be economical compared with denying several offers. This is a registered design rationale, not a universal minimum-denial theorem: a hit after the victim's final offer costs nothing. λ=1 and result-delivery timing materially change that economy. E8's companion result makes swapping in one-offer disruption consequential, not a harmless cleanup. [Family freeze, N8-8/N8-12](V6_E8_FAMILY_FREEZE.md), [active-sensing design review](V6_E8_ACTIVE_SPATIAL_SENSING_DESIGN_REVIEW.md).

Whole-tick denial also risks removing a player's opportunity to respond and making outcome depend on tick phase and callback availability. E7's ADAPT failure is a concrete warning. E8 retains phase-sensitive members, including STRESS8's absolute-tick damage check; the absence of a flagged E8 transition is not evidence that all policies are scheduler-safe. Human usefulness therefore remains an open product question alongside its demonstrated role in this ecology.

Long-lived silent memory can support intelligible stale contacts, but can also conceal why attacks miss or why reacquisition suddenly becomes expensive. Knowledge freshness and result latency need explanation. Search reach, the fixed 512-cell arena, window size and exact core assumptions constrain transfer. Seed inference is still a promotion blocker: the experimental family forbids reading `context.seed`, while the public API exposes it. Research blinding is not a product defense against reconstructing opponents' initial locations.

### Scaffolding versus mechanics

The eleven-member census, opaque fixture identities, deterministic tiling around own core, fixed search phases, callback counters, fixed draw order, anchor/core inference conventions, frozen thresholds, private corpus and bound traces make the experiment auditable. They are not a ready player-facing strategy vocabulary or product settings panel. Remembered contacts and paid sensing are potentially general mechanics; this exact family traversal and ADAPT8 switch rule are implementations of research policies.

For an adaptation test, preserve **both** the chosen E8 treatment and its parent controls as evidence-bearing environments: active/passive semantics, knowledge updates, sensing window/reach, arena, Q=8, K=1, chunk=2 rotating forward order, core/anchor geometry, disruption, action/result delivery and scoring. Preserve the frozen family and analyzers verbatim. Any future new policies must be separate artifacts. Changing sensing price, lockout, memory, capture threshold or scheduler while improving the agent would lose the attribution this review recommends. This does not require all future questions to keep these mechanics forever; it identifies what the next agent-capacity question should hold fixed.

## 8. Requirement C: evidence and competing explanations

### What ADAPT8 actually tested

ADAPT8 begins as a repeating verifier. After two consecutive observed verifications confirm its selected anchor, it switches **once** to the once-acquisition mode for the remainder of the match. An unobserved tick neither advances nor resets the quiet count. A relocation observed before the switch resets that count. This is one irreversible choice about verification allocation, not a general offense/defense controller or a policy that can resume adaptive monitoring after switching. [Family freeze, BEH-10](V6_E8_FAMILY_FREEZE.md).

The registered allocation check passed: V(EVADE8)=70 while the static-opponent values were at most 2. ADAPT8 therefore exhibited the required allocation contrast. Its U=287/352 equaled REACQ8's mixed-field U; RUSH8's U was 9/11. Neither the support nor the refutation stability condition resolved the registered primary comparison. The {EVADE8} census bounds the C interpretation, while U is still the registered mixed-field average over the eleven opponents; it is not an evader-only payoff statistic. [E8 results](V6_E8_RESULTS.md), [H8-ADAPT definition](V6_E8_ACTIVE_SPATIAL_SENSING_PREREGISTRATION.md).

### Explanations to keep open, not findings to invent

| Possible explanation | What the existing record permits |
|---|---|
| **Weak adaptive policy** | Plausible design inference: a one-way quiet switch may be an inadequate controller. NEITHER does not diagnose weakness. A named ADAPT member is not evidence of general adaptive competence. |
| **Insufficient observation signal** | SENSE can report location and change, and allocation variation occurred. Whether that signal forecasts the payoff of switching, in time to act, is unestablished. Silent evasion and lockout-delayed callbacks can limit observability. |
| **Switching/information costs exceed benefit** | Verification consumes offers, and less acquisition sometimes wins. There is no registered decomposition attributing ADAPT8's outcome to switching cost. The mode change itself has no separately charged switch action; avoid inventing one. |
| **Too little state-dependent choice** | Only EVADE8 belongs to the natural relocation census. Most members do not move under active sensing, and cores stay fixed. This is a narrow environment for testing changing responses, not proof that no such choice exists. |
| **Fixed strategies approximate useful responses** | REACQ8 already reacts to missing anchors without changing its repeating policy. It matches ADAPT8's mixed-field payoff. This makes the capacity question sharper but does not prove fixed policies are generally sufficient. |
| **An economy/resource state is required** | No registered test establishes that necessity. Existing action scarcity, denial and evasion already create repeated value. Persistent investment might create additional choices; it would also change the environment before the current one has received a capable policy test. |
| **Agent quality rather than mechanic capacity was tested** | Supported as a limitation: H8-ADAPT evaluates one frozen controller against two fixed acquisition policies. It cannot quantify over all capable observation-driven controllers. |
| **Census or adaptation dimension too narrow** | Direct scope limitation: one natural evader and one verification dimension. Offense/defense allocation, opponent inference and reversible monitoring were not established by this hypothesis. Broader tests would be new research. |

The freeze also records a possible startup effect: a scripted evader that moved only after ADAPT8's second verification could allow an early switch. It was accepted without probing against EVADE8. It remains a recorded possibility, **not** this review's causal explanation of E8 outcomes. No new individual-match inspection or empirical failure analysis was performed. [Family freeze, N8-10](V6_E8_FAMILY_FREEZE.md).

The narrow unresolved strategic question is therefore: **can a capable policy use observable changes to improve action allocation within a match on the established E8 structure, relative to matched fixed policies?** C is the priority, not the only remaining product gate. Broader interaction, seed exposure and legibility remain open even if C succeeds.

## 9. Branch A reassessment

Branch A proper means adaptive agents under the current stable-equivalent rules. It has never been disproven. E6's universal control policy and early forced-line concern were limitations of that family and an argument about research priority, not a theorem that stable Bytefray cannot support adaptation. Closed 4B–4D evidence cannot supply the missing demonstration.

The existing review distinguishes **Branch A′**, improving adaptivity under an already characterized research mechanic. Before E8 it was a fallback: the more fundamental question was whether acquisition could have a better independent channel, and E6's candidate was rejected. E8 answered that channel question positively and supplied a candidate clearing the registered pathology screen. That changes the priority: Branch A′ on **E8**, rather than on rejected E6, is now the narrowest direct route to C. [Mechanic-family review §§D, L–M](V6_E8_MECHANIC_FAMILY_DESIGN_REVIEW.md).

Improve and test the adaptive policy before changing the mechanic again. This is a recommendation about causal scope, not a guarantee that a better agent will win. Any future comparison must distinguish adaptation from generic implementation strength and fitting to the published family. It should test observations available during play rather than opponent identity, seed reconstruction or fixture fingerprints. Failure of one new policy would still bound the tested policy class, not disprove Branch A or force a new economy.

## 10. Economic-triangle and resource discussion

The greed–rush–defense triangle is a useful design hypothesis, not a law and not an E6/E8 registered result. GREED, attackers and guards exist in these families, but names do not establish the cycle greed loses to rush, rush to defense, defense to greed. A nonconstant best-response map also does not require that particular three-way cycle.

| Proposed ingredient | What Bytefray currently provides |
|---|---|
| Investable action economy | Offers can be allocated to acquisition, painting, denial, core attack or repair. There is no established banked resource producing future capacity. |
| Delayed payoff | Painting contributes later score and information can enable later actions; these differ from a compounding economic investment. Its necessity for C is untested. |
| Defense | Repair/guarding exist, with evidence both of useful posture and of immunity/stasis risk under some K/λ combinations. |
| Information cost | Explicitly demonstrated acquisition tradeoffs; repeat value in the primary evasion ecology. |
| Disruption | Action denial supplies tempo and conditional value, with schedule-sensitive costs. |
| Relocation | Evasion makes anchor information stale; owned cores remain fixed. |
| Attack tempo | Attack competes with other offers, and lockout can deny future offers. No registered greed/rush/defense economy is inferred. |

These ingredients are sufficient to justify testing capable adaptation before adding resources. They are not evidence that a durable economic triangle already exists. A resource layer could eventually supply commitment, delayed returns and changing marginal values, but would simultaneously add persistent state, balance parameters, new explanations and possible accumulation or repair immunity. At present it is premature and too broad as the first response to ADAPT8 NEITHER. A convincing need would be a bounded design argument or wider capability-controlled evidence that the available observations/actions do not offer exploitable state-dependent choices—not merely another unsuccessful named adaptive agent.

## 11. Candidate next directions

The five alternatives below are analyzed before ranking. Costs describe incremental research and qualification burden, not a claim that policy work is trivial. New mechanics preserve historical findings as constraints and controls; they do not automatically inherit E8's numerical payoffs, flags or qualification.

### 1. Stronger adaptive policy over unchanged E8 mechanics

- **Mechanism:** Observation-conditioned allocation between monitoring, reacquisition and productive actions; potentially revisit allocation when evidence changes rather than only switching once.
- **Gap addressed:** Directly tests beneficial within-match policy change without introducing another engine mechanic.
- **Evidence preserved:** E8's channel, cost and parent contrasts remain usable environments; E6's fixed-choice result and E2–E5 pathology warnings remain historical constraints. Frozen agents are not edited.
- **Primary risk:** A generally better implementation or hindsight fit beats weaker baselines and is mislabeled adaptive benefit. Matched adaptation-disabled capability and fixed alternatives are essential conceptual controls.
- **Research cost:** **LOW** relative to a new mechanic; policy discipline and qualification are still substantial.
- **Product relevance:** Lets authors express when information is worth refreshing with the existing action vocabulary.

### 2. Opponent-model inference and disruption/evasion-frequency adaptation

- **Mechanism:** Infer behavior from observations available during a match, then adjust sensing, denial or attack allocation to the observed frequency and reliability of evasion/disruption.
- **Gap addressed:** Tests whether identification and learning yield policy changes beyond the single quiet-count dimension.
- **Evidence preserved:** Existing E8 actions and mechanics can remain fixed; repeat and lower-acquisition contrasts provide premises, not the answer.
- **Primary risk:** Script fingerprints or public payoff-table memorization masquerade as state inference. Also risks adding estimator quality and policy quality as two unresolved variables.
- **Research cost:** **MEDIUM**.
- **Product relevance:** Responsive authored agents and understandable decisions such as monitoring an opponent that repeatedly relocates.

### 3. Stateful offense/defense investment or explicit resource allocation

- **Mechanism:** Allocate a finite resource or productive offers toward offense/defense state with delayed, competing returns; no free extra action capacity is assumed.
- **Gap addressed:** Could create changing marginal values and commitment, making switching depend on accumulated state rather than contact alone.
- **Evidence preserved:** Information remains a candidate separate cost channel; E3/E5 constrain immunity and repair designs. Historical results remain valid, but a changed economy requires fresh qualification.
- **Primary risk:** Repair immunity, runaway investment, hidden persistence and multiple simultaneous balance variables obscure whether adaptation or an overpowered investment wins.
- **Research cost:** **HIGH**.
- **Product relevance:** Strategic commitments and visible risk/reward, if a need beyond current allocation is established.

### 4. Limited-duration strategic modes

- **Mechanism:** Temporarily commit to competing capabilities, with a finite duration or clearly defined cost to changing mode.
- **Gap addressed:** Could expose a legible within-match choice between specialized actions when the opponent's state changes.
- **Evidence preserved:** Paid information and contact/denial loops can inform the design; it must not silently change their prices or inherit their qualification.
- **Primary risk:** Mode boundaries become another absolute-tick/callback artifact, or one mode dominates and removes the intended opportunity cost.
- **Research cost:** **MEDIUM**.
- **Product relevance:** Clear, visual choices for authors and spectators, provided mode effects and duration are understandable.

### 5. Contestable valuable locations or heterogeneous objectives

- **Mechanism:** Introduce visible objectives whose control or position changes the relative value of movement, information and attack.
- **Gap addressed:** Could broaden state-dependent decisions beyond locating one evader and attacking fixed cores.
- **Evidence preserved:** E8 justifies acquisition independent of movement; E2–E5 warn against winner privilege generated by geometry or resolution order.
- **Primary risk:** Scoring/geometry confounds, uncontested farming, avoidance and tick-limit play. Several new variables could replace rather than clarify the adaptation question.
- **Research cost:** **HIGH**.
- **Product relevance:** More readable spatial goals and changing priorities, if contact and counterplay survive.

**Ranking after analysis:** 1 is the narrowest first direction. 2 is a promising subsequent policy direction, or a carefully bounded component of 1 if its estimator is kept attributable. 4, 3 and 5 are conditional alternatives, in that order of incremental scope; none is currently justified as the next experiment. This is an ordering of research burden and attribution, not evidence that the new mechanics are strategically inferior. Visual exploration can inform F separately without claiming C.

## 12. Recommended next research/design step

**BRANCH A EXPERIMENT NEXT — Branch A′ on the existing E8 research mechanics.** This recommends an agent-capacity question, not immediate experimental execution. **Do not preregister E9 yet.** First have the lead review this synthesis; then, if authorized, undertake a focused adaptation design review. E9 should exist only if that review can make a capable-policy comparison interpretable without changing the mechanic and without fitting to the sealed fixtures.

The experiment concept is limited to:

- **Research question:** Can an observation-conditioned agent improve payoff by changing action allocation within a match on unchanged E8 mechanics?
- **Control/treatment concept:** Matched fixed allocations and an adaptation-disabled version versus a policy that changes allocation from in-match observations, sharing primitives and implementation capability.
- **Key confound:** Adaptive decision value versus generic agent strength, fixture identification or hindsight fitting. Observable allocation change and credible capability matching matter as much as payoff.
- **Material advance:** A scoped payoff advantage attributable to observation-driven policy change, alongside preserved contact and acceptable seat behavior. A negative would constrain the tested class; it would not prove that sensing lacks adaptive capacity or that resources are necessary.

There is no matrix, threshold proposal, seed protocol, preregistration, implementation plan or new policy artifact in this review. Stable-rule Branch A remains open, but the E8 environment is the cleaner present route to the specific remaining information/adaptation question. New-mechanic experiments and product promotion are not recommended at this boundary.

## 13. Product-direction implications

**Active sensing is plausible for a human-facing Ruleset.** One deliberate action buys a local snapshot and has a clear opportunity cost. E8 establishes enough strategic promise for design exploration, not default product adoption. Its reach/window, memory behavior, compatibility and exposure constraints still need product decisions.

**Whole-tick disruption is a useful candidate interaction and a research instrument.** It supplies the primary repeated-information value, so removing it could erase the demonstrated loop. Its denial severity and scheduler dependence make it the least settled part of the human game. E8 does not validate it as enjoyable, comprehensible or robust against capable opponents. One-offer denial is not an evidenced equivalent replacement for this purpose.

**Reacquisition can be understandable if current location and remembered contact are distinct.** A visual concept could show a fresh sensing window, a dated or faded remembered anchor, relocation, the disruption target, and the offers spent or denied. It should distinguish a relocated anchor from a fixed core. These are explanation proposals, not validated UI requirements or implementations.

**Replay dramatics require truthful data.** A canonical replay reconstructs engine-owned state at tick granularity; it does not record every ObservationV2, SENSE result or the policy's remembered contacts. Exact callback knowledge and timing belong in optional diagnostic traces. A future presentation should use those bound artifacts for a knowledge overlay rather than infer what an agent knew from end-of-tick anchors. A sensing pulse without reliable action/observation evidence could tell the wrong story. [Replay schema boundary](../../REPLAY_SCHEMA.md#python-observation-capture-explicitly-out-of-scope), [Agent API](../../AGENT_API_V2.md#research-only-extension-sense), [E8 trace/family freeze](V6_E8_FAMILY_FREEZE.md).

**Keep the action language/API simple while testing policy capacity.** E8 needed an additive, Ruleset-gated action and observation reflection, not a new API generation, to demonstrate the channel and repeated value. That favors trying capable policies with the existing vocabulary before adding a large resource language. It does not establish that current agent capabilities or tools are sufficient for every future mechanic. SENSE remains research-only; ordinary product Rulesets reject it.

**Do not expose research machinery as player features.** Fixed fixture identities, the EVADE8 census, bootstrap/kill thresholds, deterministic family search schedules, private corpus structure and experimental qualification toggles are research controls. Seed-derived opponent inference is a blocker, not a supported strategy to normalize through UI. Public research seed revelation does not settle runtime information exposure. Product promotion needs those exposure/capability and compatibility gates resolved, plus broader competitive and human evidence.

Promising visual prototypes are the paid sensing snapshot, stale contact memory, evasion/reacquisition sequence and action-denial budget. They could address F while the policy-capacity question remains open. No prototype was produced here, and such a prototype would not qualify the candidate competitively.

## 14. Questions still genuinely unresolved

1. Can a capable, matched policy exploit observations to change allocation profitably within a match, beyond one irreversible verification switch?
2. Does that benefit transfer beyond the one natural-evader census and the published scripted signatures?
3. How much recurring information value survives other disruption prices and schedules? The existing two-parent comparison bounds the current answer; it does not supply a general response curve.
4. Can contact, counterplay and player agency remain strong against more capable denial, repair and evasion policies?
5. Can seat/timing behavior remain acceptable when policy schedules are no longer the frozen family's schedules?
6. What exact information/exposure contract closes product seed inference without changing the demonstrated acquisition premise?
7. Can authors and spectators correctly explain fresh versus stale contacts, callback latency, core position and action cost using available artifacts?
8. If current mechanics cannot support useful adaptation for a well-defined policy class, what specific missing state would an investment, mode or objective mechanic supply?

Questions 1–2 have the strongest claim on the next strategic review. Questions 4–7 remain independent product gates. None authorizes reopening sealed results or tuning agents against private E8 trajectories.
